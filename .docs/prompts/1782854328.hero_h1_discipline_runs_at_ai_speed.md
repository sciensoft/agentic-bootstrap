Two corrections to the H1, both flagged in one user turn:

1. *"So does the discipline"* (committed in the previous turn) is wrong. The discipline didn't change — it just couldn't be done at pre-AI manual speed. 2. With β as the new line 4, the H1 sentence is now too long to fit one line at the current font tier (`text-5xl md:text-6xl lg:text-7xl`) inside the `lg:col-span-7` container. Shrink the H1 one tier (to `text-4xl md:text-5xl lg:text-6xl`) rather than break β across two lines or shorten it to a 4-word slogan. ## Reasoning

The previous H1 line 4 (*"So does the discipline"*) was correct on protagonist but implied a category-parallel change — software engineering changed, *and the discipline also changed*. That's not the actual claim. The discipline (ADRs, prompts, security rubrics, conventions, the whole engineering rigor canon) is decades old. It didn't evolve in response to AI. What changed is that **applying it consistently used to be impossible at human speed and is now possible at AI speed because the bootstrap automates its application at every commit.** The product solves a *delivery* problem, not a *substance* problem.

The right second sentence names that delivery shift. *"The discipline runs at AI speed."* uses two carrier verbs — *runs* implies automation (the discipline executes, doesn't just sit in a doc), and *at AI speed* explicitly names the new pace dimension the previous line elided. Reader's inference path: *engineering changed (got abundant and fast) → the same discipline now keeps up because it runs at the new pace*. The bootstrap is the unspoken mechanism; the lede and subhead name it.

The visual fit problem is real and orthogonal to the content. At `text-7xl` (4.5rem ≈ 72px) with `font-extrabold tracking-tight`, a 5-word / 28-character sentence runs ~1100px wide — far past the `lg:col-span-7` container's effective ~720px width on desktop. The H1 would wrap silently, breaking the rhythm. Three resolutions were offered (break across 2 lines / shorten to slogan / shrink font tier); the chosen path (shrink tier) keeps the 4-line hero structure intact and keeps the full sentence on one line. Cost: the staircased *Software · engineering · changed.* lines lose some visual gravity at the smaller tier. Benefit: nothing wraps; the H1 reads as one unit at every screen size; the comparison of two sentences (cause / resolution) lands cleanly.

ADR-0010's H1 description is updated to record both the new copy and the type-tier shrink, plus the third H1 draft (*"So does the discipline"*) added to the withdrawn-drafts history alongside the two from the prior turn.
