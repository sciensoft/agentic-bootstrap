# Request

Three corrections applied to the R1 hero in a single commit:

1. **The subhead was a copy-paste, not a rewrite.** The previous turn swapped one word (*discipline* → *engineering memory*) but kept the pre-AI metaphor (*"a Senior Engineer would have set up on day one"*) and the list of file artifacts (*workflow rules, ADR culture, testing pyramid, telemetry, security rubric, TODO discipline*) — both of which actively contradict the new positioning. Genuinely rewrite under the brief.
2. *"Repositories haven't"* puts the **repository in the protagonist role**, when the protagonist is the human–AI collaboration and the repo is the setting/canvas. The H1 needs a subject that isn't the repo.
3. The H1's line 4 should be **positive and catchy** — what the product *delivers*, not what's missing. Example: *"The discipline hasn't"* (gap form) → restate as the bootstrap's positive resolution.

Also: it's acceptable to keep the v1 subhead's *"Your repo grows the discipline"* central clause, since *"your repo"* in supporting copy reads as a possessed instrument, not as a competing protagonist.

## Reasoning

This turn earned three corrections in sequence because I'd been treating the brief as a checklist rather than a structural argument. The brief's diagram (*Engineer ↔ Agentic AI → Repository*) makes the role distribution explicit: the **engineer + AI** is the *protagonist* (the cognitive system producing engineering knowledge); the **repository** is the *setting/canvas* (the venue that does or doesn't hold what's produced); the **bootstrap** is the *enabling tool* (the operating model that makes the holding happen). When *"Repositories haven't"* appears as the H1's line 4, the repository is grammatically promoted to the protagonist role — the actor that is failing to act — which (a) puts the wrong noun in the subject slot and (b) is a gap-statement when the line should be a positive resolution showing what the bootstrap delivers.

**The fix is the discipline** as both the noun and the subject. Under the new positioning, *"the discipline"* names what the bootstrap installs (the rigor, the rules, the memory practices) and it's a noun that *can change in parallel to software engineering itself* — which is what produces the line-4 mirror. *"Software engineering changed. So does the discipline."* — the elided verb in *"so does"* echoes *"changed"*; the parallel rhythm lands the resolution without restating it; the protagonist of both sentences is an *abstraction of the practice*, not a *container*. Two alternative landings were considered and rejected: *"The discipline catches up"* (correct but slightly verbose — *catches up* explicitly tells the reader the discipline was behind, which is true but less elegant than letting the parallel imply it); *"Now the discipline does too"* (correct but loses the rhythmic compression of *"so does"*).

**The subhead's protagonist rule is different from the H1's** — and the user clarified this as part of the correction. In the main hero beats (eyebrow, H1, lede), where the reader's attention concentrates and noun choices set the framing, the repository cannot occupy the actor slot. In supporting copy (subhead, body sections), the repository can appear as a *possessed subject* (*"your repo grows the discipline"*) because readers don't parse a possessed instrument as a competing protagonist — *"your"* signals tool-you-own, not actor-who-acts-upon-you. This is now codified in ADR-0010 as the *Protagonist convention* so it doesn't have to be re-discovered for every future copy edit.

**The subhead's substance, not just its verb, needed rebuilding.** The pre-AI metaphor (*Senior Engineer would have set up on day one*) explicitly contradicts the brief's central claim — that agentic AI now produces *more* engineering knowledge than any solo human could. The file-artifact list (*workflow rules, ADR culture, testing pyramid, …*) is the brief's literal *never-lead-with* list. Replaced: the central sentence now reads *"Your repo grows the discipline that holds every session's engineering — architectural decisions, the reasoning behind them, deferred ideas, conventions, security context — so the next session walks in with all of it."* The verb *"holds"* names what the discipline does. The categories of knowledge (decisions, reasoning, deferred ideas, conventions, security context) name what the discipline holds — as *kinds of engineering knowledge*, not as filenames. The closing payoff (*"the next session walks in with all of it"*) names the inheritance loop that resolves the H1's setup. The discipline-as-noun unifies the H1, the subhead, and (downstream) the body sections to come.
