# Request

Insert a small *Aha* moment between the hero and the *60-second demo* section. Named *"Why this matters?"* Copy:

> While working with an agentic AI, you're constantly creating engineering knowledge.
>
> - Architectural decisions.
> - Trade-offs.
> - Future ideas.
> - Conventions.
>
> Things worth keeping.
>
> Most of it disappears.
>
> AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP keeps it.

Constraint: *tiny*. Not another hero, not another essay. Just enough to make the demo obvious.

## Reasoning

Under [ADR-0010](../adrs/0010-reposition-as-engineering-operating-model.md)'s landing-page structure plan, R2 was scoped as *"Add What just changed in software engineering + The new bottleneck — body prose only, no artifact names"* at ~250 words. In practice the beat needed to lead into the 60-second demo — not compete with it. The user's brief here compresses R2 into a single Aha section: the four categories of engineering knowledge listed as chips (which mirror ADR-0010's subhead-mapped-to-substrate-artifacts hierarchy — *architecture → ADRs, decisions → prompts, trade-offs → ADR consequences, future ideas → `.docs/todos/`* — but without saying it here); a three-line resolution that dips into the *disappears* problem and lands on *AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP keeps it*. §2 and §3 of the R2 plan (*What just changed* + *The new bottleneck*) collapse into this one beat because the demo section that follows carries the concrete affordance; a longer body-prose interstitial would push the demo below the fold on many viewports.

Visual shape chosen from three options (chip row / two-column contrast / motion-driven fade-and-restore) — user picked the *chip row* recommendation. Rationale: the chip row reuses the hero eyebrow pattern (same `rounded-full border border-white/10 bg-white/5` idiom), doesn't compete with the terminal typewriter's motion story, and stays within the *tiny* constraint. The other two options are documented in the AskUserQuestion turn preceding this request.
