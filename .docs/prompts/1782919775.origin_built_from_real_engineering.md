# Request

Add a new *"Built from real engineering"* section that tells the origin story of AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP — why it exists, in the maker's voice. Placement: **after the 60-second demo, before "What you get"**, so the reader hits it at the *"wait, who built this and why?"* moment right after seeing the mechanism. Copy (verbatim):

> AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP wasn't born from trying to build a product. It was born from trying not to repeat myself.
>
> It started while building a project where I gradually developed a better way of collaborating with agentic AI. As the project evolved, so did the engineering: architectural decisions, TODOs, conventions, prompt history, ADRs, and the operating model itself.
>
> Then I started another project.
>
> Almost immediately I found myself thinking:
>
> > "I wish this project had everything I built into the last one."
>
> Instead, I was rebuilding the same engineering practices from scratch. Repeating conversations with the AI. Rediscovering conventions. Losing valuable ideas because they lived only inside the chat. Avoiding clearing the context because I didn't want to lose the reasoning that got us there.
>
> By the third project, it was obvious.
>
> This shouldn't be something I rebuilt every time.
> It should be something I bootstrap once and evolve forever.
>
> That's why AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP exists.
>
> It gives every new project the engineering maturity of the previous one from day one.
>
> Start with the bootstrap.
> Keep what works.
> Remove what doesn't.
> Add your own skills, workflows, standards, and conventions.
> Evolve it with your project.
>
> AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP isn't the operating model. It's the starting point for yours.

## Reasoning

The reader has just seen *how* AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP works via the 60-second demo — that's the natural moment where the *"who built this and why?"* question lands. Inserting the origin story here converts curiosity into trust before the reader dives into the deeper feature exploration below (What you get → Features → Runtime footprint → Architectures → Postures). The alternative placement (right before *Adoption* at line 1171) would put the origin story ~700 lines further down; readers who bounced mid-page never see it, and the trust beat lands after the emotional-commitment window has already closed.

The story's closing positioning line (*"AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP isn't the operating model. It's the starting point for yours."*) also sets up *What you get* naturally — the reader arrives at the artifact list with the *"and you make it your own"* framing already primed.

## Design choices worth noting

- **Two gradient brand mentions, not three.** The section names AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP three times: opening (*"wasn't born..."*), mid (*"That's why AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP exists"*), and closing (*"isn't the operating model"*). The opening is plain; the mid and closing use gradient. Rationale: gradient at the opening would front-load brand attention over the personal narrative the paragraph is establishing; keeping the opening plain lets the reader hear the maker's voice before the brand asserts itself. Both later mentions are moments of positioning weight — that's where the brand pop earns its space.
- **Blockquote uses HTML curly-quote entities (`&ldquo;`/`&rdquo;`) rather than straight quotes.** Matches the story's role as a spoken thought — typographic quotes read as *this is what I said to myself*, not *this is a code identifier*. Elsewhere on the page (e.g., the hero terminal) uses straight quotes because those are literal typed text.
- **Action stanza uses `<p>` elements inside `space-y-1`, not `<ul><li>`.** Semantically the five lines are a manifesto stanza, not enumerated items. `<ul>` would add bullet markers or require `list-style: none` overrides; `<p>` renders exactly as intended without style overrides and reads naturally as prose rhythm.
- **`items-center` is NOT used** (unlike the Aha bridge's grid) because the whole section is a single-column reading flow — no grid, no vertical-alignment concerns.
