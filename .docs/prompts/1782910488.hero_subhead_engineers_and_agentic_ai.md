# Request

Update the hero subhead ([`docs/index.html`](../../docs/index.html)) from:

> The fastest engineering conversations now happen between **humans and AI**. AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP **makes sure** the architecture, decisions, trade-offs, and future ideas **survive long after the session ends**.

to:

> The fastest engineering conversations now happen between **engineers and agentic AI**. AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP **continuously turns** their architecture, decisions, trade-offs, and future ideas into **lasting project knowledge**—**without breaking the flow**.

## Reasoning

Four sharpenings against the previous subhead draft:

1. **humans → engineers.** *Humans* is broad and includes non-engineers (PMs, designers, students). The hero's audience is engineers writing production code; the subject noun should say so.
2. **AI → agentic AI.** Echoes the eyebrow (*Agentic AI changed how we build software.*) and disambiguates from chat-window Copilot-style tools that aren't the referent — the substrate exists because agents *act on the codebase*, not because engineers *chat with a model*.
3. **makes sure … survive → continuously turns … into lasting project knowledge.** Passive-guardian framing → active present-continuous framing. The product is *doing the work* every commit, not standing watch after the fact. *Lasting project knowledge* names the output as a category engineers already have a mental model for; the durability meaning is preserved in *lasting* without the session-ends framing (implicit anyway).
4. **New closing beat: —without breaking the flow.** Mirrors the H1's *Keep the flow, and the engineering.* The reader arrives at the subhead's end with the H1's promise made concrete: the discipline that has to survive doesn't tax the tempo. Em-dash unspaced (matches the user's markdown) for a snap cadence — spaced would breathe where the beat wants to punch.

The compound-antecedent *their* in *"turns their architecture, decisions, trade-offs, and future ideas"* refers back to *engineers and agentic AI* as a collective — the outputs belong to the collaboration, not to either party alone. Small but load-bearing framing: the substrate captures *what the pair produced together*, not what the engineer authored or what the AI generated.
