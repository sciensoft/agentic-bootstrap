# Request

Restart the landing-page positioning from first principles. The current framing ("repositories should remember the engineering") still leads with the *consequence*; the underlying *category shift* is that agentic AI now produces more engineering knowledge in an afternoon than a week of solo work, and repositories weren't built to keep any of it. Reframe the project on every public surface as **an engineering operating model for human–AI software development**, whose existence is justified by the line *"Agentic AI has made engineering knowledge abundant. AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP makes it durable."* Replace the landing-page hero with a single curiosity-led variant that creates the gap rather than answering it; keep the A/B/C engine intact for future variants. Preserve the *Runtime footprint* section and reposition it under the new spine — it answers the cost objection the new framing will provoke. Do not push the commit once done.

## Reasoning

ADR-0009 (the previous repositioning) reframed the project as an *engineering-memory system* and ran an A/B/C hero test, currently pinned to variant B. A subsequent product-strategy review flagged that even the new framing describes the *consequence* (repos should remember) rather than the *underlying problem* (a category-level shift in software engineering). Repositories have always failed to remember engineering; that fact doesn't explain why now is the moment to fix it. What's new is that human–AI collaboration generates engineering knowledge at a rate repositories were never designed to absorb. The implication: the homepage shouldn't begin by explaining the bootstrap; it should begin by explaining what just changed in software engineering, naming the new bottleneck (retention, not creation), drawing the IaC / GitOps / containerization parallel, and only then introducing the artifact substrate as evidence.

Three forces shape the chosen scope for R1:

1. The brief is a **full restart**, not an extension. The previous 8-phase backlog ([`landing-positioning-restructure.md`](../todos/landing-positioning-restructure.md)) built around "five memory types ↔ artifacts" no longer fits — that framing is still mechanism wearing a metaphor costume. The TODO is removed and replaced with a new 7-commit plan reflecting the new spine.
2. The A/B/C engine is **kept, but collapsed to a single variant** ('A'). The infrastructure introduced in ADR-0009 (synchronous `<head>` IIFE, `localStorage` stickiness, bot pinning, `gtag` instrumentation, CSS `data-hero-variant-block` attribute hide-show, `FORCE_VARIANT` override) costs ~50 lines and is exactly what a future multi-arm test will need; deleting it would force re-writing it later. Only the variant **blocks** in `<body>` collapse; `pickWeighted()` is reduced to `return 'A'` with a comment showing how to restore weights.
3. *Runtime footprint* is **preserved and slated for repositioning in R6** (not R1). Under the new framing it becomes a critical proof point — *the substrate is durable AND doesn't tax every session* — directly answering the implicit objection the philosophical sections will provoke ("won't this cost a fortune in tokens?"). Removing it would weaken the new positioning, not strengthen it.

R1 (this commit) lands the hero + the planning artifacts. R2–R7 (separate commits) carry the philosophical body sections, the substrate-as-evidence reframe, the workflow vignette, the *Runtime footprint* reframe, the comparison-vs-other-tools section, and the README pivot. The OG/meta tags are intentionally NOT updated in R1 — they currently encode ADR-0009's variant-B copy; touching them in R1 alone would mismatch the live page mid-restructure. They sweep in R3.
