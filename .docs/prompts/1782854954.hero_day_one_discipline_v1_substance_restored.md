## Reasoning

Two over-corrections to walk back honestly:

**Over-correction 1 — the Senior Engineer / day-one framing.** Earlier this turn I treated *"a Senior Engineer would have set up on day one"* as a pre-AI metaphor that undersells the brief's claim about agentic AI producing more knowledge than any solo human could. That critique misread the metaphor's job. The metaphor's job is not *"this is the standard set by what one human could do"* — it's *"this is the standard a careful human would have aspired to but never could deliver consistently."* The bootstrap doesn't compete with the senior; it **delivers what the senior would have wanted, automatically, at the pace AI now demands.** So the metaphor is a *positive anchor in something the reader recognizes*, not a *backwards-looking ceiling*. Restored.

**Over-correction 2 — the file-artifact list.** I treated *"workflow rules, ADR culture, the testing pyramid, telemetry, security rubric, TODO discipline"* as a violation of the brief's *"never lead with file names"* guidance. That rule applies to **main hero beats** (eyebrow, H1, lede) where word choice sets the framing. In **supporting copy** (subhead, body sections) a concrete list of recognizable artifacts anchors the abstract discipline — the reader sees ADRs, testing pyramid, security rubric and thinks *"oh, the actual rigor of a well-run project"*, which is exactly the intended grasp. The protagonist convention (see ADR-0010) already established this main-beat vs. supporting-copy distinction; the file-list belongs on the supporting side.

**The H1 slogan structure.** *"The day-one discipline. / At Agentic AI speed."* is an *adjective-noun ↔ prepositional-phrase* parallel that reads as a slogan rather than a sentence. The two halves stack as the resolution to the staircased opener. Each half is short enough to fit on a single line at the smaller H1 tier (`text-4xl md:text-5xl lg:text-6xl`) without wrapping. The *day-one discipline* line directly echoes the v1 H1's first line — preserving the design heritage — while *at Agentic AI speed* adds the new delivery dimension the previous *"runs at AI speed"* phrasing tried to deliver in one line. Two lines = cleaner rhythm + better fit.

**The lede tightening.** *"Agentic AI **has made** engineering knowledge abundant. AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP **makes** it durable."* mixes a past-perfect verb (*has made*) with a present-tense verb (*makes*). Tightening to *"Agentic AI **makes** engineering knowledge abundant. AGENTIC_BOOTSTRAP **makes** it durable."* aligns both halves on the same present-tense parallel verb (*makes / makes*). Reads as a tighter mirror pair. Brief's tagline content unchanged.
